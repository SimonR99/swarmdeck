# Known issues

| Area | Limitation, trap, and mitigation |
|---|---|
| Merged components smear the floor and break terrain gates | With the relaxed peer admission (`similarity_threshold 0.70`, `registration_min_inliers 18`, `registration_min_overlap 0.40`) the Bistro fleet merges into one component within seconds, but the closures place platforms with different lidar heights about 0.3 m apart vertically (Spot versus Bunker at a stationary start: estimated 0.375 m against 0.088 m true), and after 20 m of drift-odometry travel the pairwise errors reach 0.45 to 0.83 m in XY, 3 to 6 degrees in heading and 0.15 to 0.25 m in Z. Each robot's product then contains its neighbours' floors 0.2 to 0.4 m above or below its own (occupied floor bands at -0.6, -0.4 and -0.2 m around one robot), so the 0.15 m step gate rejects flat road as `known rise` and Navigate and Explore fail everywhere. XY and yaw were within 6 mm and 0.01 degrees at the start; the vertical error is a registration bias between platforms, not a false match. Until inter-robot constraints are planar or vertically corrected for ground robots, the deployed override keeps the strict cslam image (no merges). The peer-body mask must stay on: without it, neighbours at the grouped start are baked into every robot's persistent map as surface samples that free-space rays never remove, and Navigate near the start fails with phantom `known rise` steps. |
| Planning map momentarily unavailable while moving | MGG drops its MOLA map the instant a new keyframe changes the authority key and serves again one to three seconds later, when the rebuilt product is loaded. A rolling route asks for its next section right after the keyframes of the section it just drove. The adapter treats `odometry or planning map is unavailable`, `MOLA map snapshot is missing or stale` and `MOLA map unavailable` as temporary: retried every 0.5 s within `authority_replan_deadline_s` (15 s) without spending planning attempts, ending with `MGG planning map stayed unavailable`. |
| A parked robot never re-observed its surroundings | Swarm-SLAM earns keyframes by distance only. A robot parked behind another had its forward floor occluded at the start and never captured the scene again after the neighbour left, so it had no measured ground ahead and could not move (the chronic robot_1 stall). `deploy/patches/cslam-scene-change-keyframe.patch` takes one more keyframe when at least `keyframe_scene_change_fraction` of 72 azimuth sectors change their minimum range by more than `keyframe_scene_change_range_m`, at most once per `keyframe_scene_change_min_period_s`. |
| Inter-robot merges are switched off | `SWARMDECK_INTER_ROBOT_CLOSURES` defaults to false in `deploy/autonomy/peer.launch.py`, which sets `frontend.inter_robot_loop_closure_budget` to 0; `deploy/patches/cslam-inter-robot-switch.patch` makes that an explicit off switch, because Swarm-SLAM's selector does not treat zero as select-nothing. Re-enable only after the vertical placement between platforms is corrected (see the merged-floor row). |
| Fleet Explore from the grouped start is unreliable | The peer-body mask removes neighbours from every map and MGG has no other knowledge of them, so first paths from the grouped start run through parked robots. DWB then improvises outside the validated corridor. With a symmetric 0.6 m/s reverse limit it swung robots through full-speed reverse arcs up to 4 m off the path and they wedged beside a 0.19 m kerb at (-9.7, 3.3) and near (-11.6, 7.6) in the simulation frame (three failed starts, 2026-09-17, ground truth and `cmd_vel` traced). `min_vel_x` is now -0.15 in `swarmdeck_nav/config/nav2_params.yaml`: one grouped start then passed for all four, the next still wedged robot_0 at the same kerb. Explore from dispersed poses passes (25 to 33 m per robot). Open: live peer exclusions in MGG route planning, and why a route MGG admits beside that kerb is not physically drivable. |
| Indexed map refused every query after each keyframe | The peer replaces `snapshot.json` first and the MOLA planner product one to three seconds later. The index server read that gap as a broken source (`MOLA index does not match current snapshot bytes`), failed closed, and every query returned `a different snapshot failed indexed publication`, which MGG reports as a stale revision and the adapter turned into `nav failed` four seconds after the goal. A pending publication (`autonomy.map_provider.PublicationPending`) now leaves the indexed publication serving its own snapshot key, bounded by the coherent-read age, with no failure backoff; the adapter also waits out the index-side stale reasons within `authority_replan_deadline_s`; the server logs the reason whenever it does fail closed. |
| Kerbs exactly at the step limit | Bistro kerbs measure 0.151 and 0.152 m against the 0.150 m step limit, so straight-ahead goals flipped between admitted and refused on millimetre noise (`known rise 0.151 m exceeds step limit 0.150 m`). MGG's footprint terrain check adds `footprint_step_measurement_tolerance_m` (default 0.01 m, clamped to 0.05 m) and reports it in the rejection text. |
| Authority tilt rejected by the planner | Before MGG `043fbd7`, the MOLA backend refused any tilt above 1e-6 rad between the authority transform's Z axis and the navigation Z axis in its footprint circle and swept cylinder queries, and the planner's indexed query had the same gate. A peer SLAM correction carries a few milliradians of pitch and roll, so a robot lost every lattice edge as `collision` the moment its component frame came from a closure. The tolerance is now 0.02 rad (`kMaxAuthorityTiltRad`); larger tilts still refuse. |
| Solver noise rebuilt the map every cycle | The peer solver re-optimizes on every accepted closure and on a timer. With an exact 1e-6 pose comparison every result was a correction: a new geometry revision, a MOLA rebuild (about one second) and a one-second window in which the planner read a snapshot without its index (`MOLA index is not coherent with the mapping snapshot`). The bridge now treats deltas under 5 mm and 0.05 degrees as unchanged (`SOLUTION_CHANGE_TRANSLATION_M`, `SOLUTION_CHANGE_ROTATION_RAD` in `autonomy/cslam.py`), and the MGG loader retries a coherence mismatch within its load budget. |
| 2D map panel waits forever in onboard mode | The 2D map panel defaults to the **Optimized** source, which is the central SLAM solver's output. In the peer Swarm-SLAM and MOLA mode nothing feeds that solver (it is diagnostics only), so the panel shows `Waiting for map` indefinitely. Switch the panel's map source to the per-robot SLAM grids (served at `/api/map`), or use the tactical map with the **Live** source, which is the intended view for onboard mapping. |
| Jazzy apt drift and cache anchors | `apt-get install` in the mapping, sim and robot-ros2 images is only reproducible from Docker's layer cache: the ROS repository no longer serves MOLA 2.9.0 (3.2.0 only), and a fresh install brings nav2 1.3.13 plus newer slam_toolbox and tf2 builds under which the simulation's lifecycle bring-up fails (`lifecycle bringup did not answer`, Nav2 action server absent). BuildKit keys every later RUN on the ARGs declared above it, so those Dockerfiles keep `ARG MGG_REV=902e868…` as a cache anchor above the apt layer and declare the real pin again before use. Do not move or change the anchor; a build on a host without the cache fails or drifts. |
| Python environment & GTSAM segfault | `slam/` is strictly pinned to Python 3.12 and `numpy<2`. GTSAM 4.2.2 segfaults with bare SIGSEGV under NumPy 2.x (Python 3.13). Never combine `server/.venv` (3.13) and `slam/.venv` (3.12). |
| GTSAM tangent vector order | Information matrices and tangent vectors in `slam/` are 6x6 **rotation first** ($\omega_x, \omega_y, \omega_z, v_x, v_y, v_z$). Ordering mistakes fail silently without errors; guarded by tests in `slam/tests/`. |
| Transform naming direction | Every transform `T_a_b` maps points from frame `b` to frame `a` ($p_a = T_{a\_b} \cdot p_b$). |
| PCM clique threshold | `min_pcm_clique_size = 2`: trajectory merging requires at least two corroborating inter-robot loop closures. A single drive-by overlap will deliberately remain unmerged until re-visited. |
| Rotated keyframe copies | Duplicate timestamps could bypass the yaw gate; capture-time TF could fall back to a later pose. Fixed at capture; existing keyframes remain unchanged. See [diagnosis and capture behavior](keyframe-yaw.md). |
| Motion skew at turn rates | In simulation, spinning lidar clouds have no per-point timestamps and distort during fast yaw (> 8 deg/s), causing GICP to lock onto warped structure. `keyframe_producer.py` drops frames above `DEFAULT_MAX_YAW_RATE = 0.14 rad/s`. |
| Exploration bootstrap vs operator goals | `explore.py` and Nav2's `velocity_smoother` publish to the same `<ns>/cmd_vel`. While both command one robot it receives interleaved, contradictory velocities and follows neither, which looks like a robot ignoring its planned path or a broken transform. Exploration now watches each robot's `navigate_to_pose` status and stops commanding any robot with an accepted or executing goal (logged as `yielding to an operator goal`). Note that `EXPLORE_SECONDS` is WALL-CLOCK by design, so a low real-time factor widens the window during which an operator may issue a goal without shortening it; `EXPLORE_SECONDS=0` disables the bootstrap. |
| Simulation reset boundary | The `reset` capability is strictly simulation-only (`adapter_sim`, `mock_adapter`). Hardware adapters (`adapter_ros1`, `adapter_ros2`) must never advertise or implement `reset`. |
| Auto map registration overhead | In `merge_mode: auto`, grid correlation costs ~2.44 s per ingest if reference robot is missing. Use `merge_mode: graph` (default in `configs/4robot.yaml` and `hardware_fleet.yaml`) for trajectory-based merging. |
| Collaborative merge (legacy cslam) | RTAB-Map grids and Swarm-SLAM trajectories disagree by 11 to 16 m. Treat `merge_mode: cslam` as a legacy diagnostic; use `merge_mode: graph` for production. |
| 3D simulation | `GRID_3D=true` preserves cloud height but roughly halves simulation speed. Leave it off unless the 3D structure is needed. |
| DLIO in Gazebo | Simulated clouds lack per-point timestamps, so DLIO cannot demonstrate de-skewing. Do not extrapolate its Gazebo result to hardware. |
| Odometry covariance | Legacy Gazebo backend only. The EKF is tuned for its current measurement model. Enabling `FUSE_COVARIANCE=true` requires retuning process noise. |
| Gazebo lidar ring parity | Legacy Gazebo backend. Height-filtered planar mapping requires 1 or an odd number of vertical rings (e.g., 33); even counts leave no beam at elevation 0 and distant walls truncate. |
| Fast DDS drops point clouds silently | A 17-ring revolution is ~630 KB and Fast DDS sizes its UDP socket buffer from `net.core.rmem_max`, 208 KB by default, so a single message does not fit, the fragments overflow, and it is never reassembled. The publisher reports success and nothing logs an error: the only symptom is Ultra-Fusion appearing to diverge. Measured upstream at 87% loss, presenting as 47% drift. `fastdds_large.xml` asks for 7 MB buffers, and the ceiling on that is a **host** setting: `sudo sysctl -w net.core.rmem_max=8388608`. It cannot be set from the container (the sysctl is global, not per-netns, and Docker refuses to create a container that asks); `run_uf.sh` reads it at startup and warns. Check `arrivals{}` in the estimator's log before touching any tuning parameter. |
| Ultra-Fusion is a third-party binary | It comes from one Docker Hub tag (`maotiandocker/ultrafusion-ros2:0.2.0`; the documented `0.2.1` does not exist) plus a GitHub release `.deb` whose declared dependencies that image does not carry. Both are worked around in `deploy/docker/ultrafusion/Dockerfile`; both are upstream artifacts that can move. |
| ARGoS runs in lockstep with the estimator | The `external_estimator` exchange blocks, so the simulation's real-time factor is bounded by Ultra-Fusion. That is deliberate (nothing is dropped however slow the estimator is), but it means a slow estimator shows up as a slow simulation rather than as missing data. |
| Photorealism entity descriptors | An entity type with no `<type>.visual.xml` in the asset search path is skipped by the renderer, matched by exact name. The robot is then invisible in RGB, depth and segmentation *and* to every other robot's lidar, which raytraces the render scene. The only sign is one `[INFO] Photorealism: no visual model for entity type` line. `make visual-test` catches it. |
| Moving objects in accumulated peer maps | Two mechanisms now handle other robots. In simulation the peer bridge drops lidar returns inside another robot's body at the capture stamp (`SWARMDECK_PEER_BODY_MASK`, ground-truth poses, enabled by `scripts/sim-up` and the benchbot override, off on hardware). For hardware and for anything the mask misses, the planner product retires an endpoint once three later qualified rays see through its voxel (see the MOLA runtime guide); retired endpoints leave the occupied set and the terrain surface samples, so a neighbour that drove away stops counting as a `known rise`. Neither removes a robot that is still standing there; live avoidance of present neighbours remains Nav2's local costmap, and feeding peer positions to MGG as exclusions is an open gate. |
| Exposure and illuminance are one setting | The photorealism medium is physically based, so `<sun intensity>` and `<exposure>` have to agree: EV = log2(N^2/t * 100/S). Pairing 70 klux with EV 10.9 returns a white frame with the geometry burned out of it, while depth and segmentation stay perfect, so nothing downstream notices the photograph is unusable. |
| ARGoS lidar ring parity | Same rule as Gazebo, different mechanism: ARGoS spreads `rings` evenly over `vertical_fov`, so an even count leaves no ring at elevation 0 and `cloud_to_scan`'s `slice` mode selects nothing at range. `configs/*.yaml` use 17 rings over +/-15 deg, which is VLP-16 geometry plus a horizontal ring. |
| Ground plane and floor slab must not coincide | The `<jolt>` engine supplies the ground as a `<floor height="0">` plane. `indoor.gltf` carries a floor slab too, whose top face is also at z=0, because the renderer needs a surface to photograph. Give the physics mesh both and every robot rests on two coincident surfaces: measured, the fleet kept 0-40% of its commanded turn rate (varying with arena position, all three platforms) while translation was unaffected, so it presents as robots that drive but will not turn, or as one platform's controller being broken. Nothing logs it. `make_argos_world.py` therefore writes a second, floorless `indoor_collision.gltf` for the `<mesh>`, and `make_argos_session.py` points physics at it while the `<prop>` keeps drawing `indoor.gltf`. Tests in `test_make_argos_session.py` assert the two carry the same transform and different files, and `test_argos_headless.sh` asserts the collision copy stays the smaller of the two. With the slab removed the fleet turns at 90-100% of commanded, measured on all three platforms at three arena positions. |
| Unix socket path length | The three simulation containers share `/run/swarmdeck`, and it is short on purpose: a Unix socket path over 107 bytes fails to bind with "AF_UNIX path too long", named in whichever process binds first and not in the one that configured it. |
| Simulated detection targets are placeholders | The five models in `argos/assets/props/` are procedural, built from the catalog's own prompt descriptions rather than photographed objects. Recall against them is not evidence about the hardware catalog; re-run `tests/perception/test_catalog_recall.py` on real fixtures before drawing conclusions. |
| Detection prompts | YOLOE results depend on prompt and hardware domain. Validate catalog changes with `tests/perception/test_catalog_recall.py` inside the detector container. |
| Hardware frames | Camera/lidar extrinsics must be measured. Botman intentionally refuses deployment without its six OAK mount values. |
| ROS domains | Keep each hardware stack on its documented `ROS_DOMAIN_ID` unless the entire graph is reconfigured together. |
| Remote access | SwarmDeck has no authentication. Put an authenticating proxy in front of any hardware-facing deployment. |
| **`scan_ring` lives outside this repo** | `/ouster/scan` is built from ONE lidar beam, chosen by `scan_ring` in `<mist_ws>/src/control/rover_launch/config/drivers/driver_ouster.yaml`: a per-robot ROS workspace, **not** in this repository, so a SwarmDeck deploy can never fix it. The stock value `0` is the TOPMOST beam (+44.65 deg measured on Botman): the "laser scan" is a ring of ceiling returns at slant range, which Nav2 reads as a wall ~2.7 m away in every direction and which blocks every nav goal past ~2.6 m. Use the ring nearest 0 deg: on Botman's 64-ring sensor that is **31** (+0.81 deg); prefer slightly upward so it can never give false floor returns. Verify with `ros2 topic echo /ouster/scan`: a ceiling ring has ALL beams returning with tiny stdev (~0.15 m); a real scan varies (measured 4.49 m stdev, 38 m max). Changing it needs a **lidar restart**. |
| **ASLAN IS NOT PATCHED (2026-08-25)** | Botman has `scan_ring: 31` applied in both its `src/` and `install/` copies. **Aslan still has `scan_ring: 0`** and was powered down for the night before it could be done. It is the same Bunker chassis and the same lidar, so it needs BOTH fixes before it can navigate: (1) `scan_ring: 0` -> `31` in its own `mist_ws` copy (file is root-owned; `src/` is user-writable, and `install/` can be updated by copying through a container since the workspace is mounted read-only into the lidar container), then restart the lidar; (2) the `obstacle_min_range` change in the row below is already in this repo but has NOT been deployed to Aslan: it needs a nav2 rebuild. Use `docker compose ... up -d --no-deps --force-recreate <service>`; `scripts/deploy` (even `--no-reset`) runs `up -d --force-recreate` on ALL services and will reset SLAM. |
| Lidar sees the robot's own chassis | Once `/ouster/scan` is genuinely horizontal, the beam hits the Bunker's own body (1.02 x 0.78 m, half-diagonal ~0.64 m). Measured by sweeping headings: returns at 0.58-0.65 m in EVERY direction, a near-uniform ring the obstacle layer marks as real and inflation seals into a wall around the robot: every goal past ~1 m fails in every direction. `obstacle_min_range` / `raytrace_min_range` are therefore **0.85** on both costmaps. This stays hidden while `scan_ring` is wrong, because a beam aimed at the ceiling never has anything within 0.45 m. A near-uniform ring across all headings means self-returns, not a room. |
| Global costmap must not roll | `global_costmap.rolling_window` must be `false`. A rolling window follows the robot and caps planning at its own width/height no matter how large the map is: measured against 66.0 x 58.4 m maps with a 40 x 40 m window, every goal past ~20 m had no cells to search. Nothing errors: the LOCAL costmap (correctly rolling) keeps working, so it presents as "local navigation works but I cannot get a full path". `MapService.nav_grid` serves a robot's own grid while unmerged, so a static map always exists. Asserted by `swarmdeck_ros/src/swarmdeck_bringup/test/test_costmap_sources.py`. |
| Inflation radius below the footprint | `inflation_radius: 0.50` is smaller than the Bunker's circumscribed radius (0.766 m), so cells 0.50-0.77 m from an obstacle read cost 0 while the chassis corner can still reach them. Nav2 measures `inflation_radius` from the obstacle, not as clearance beyond the footprint. Latent collision risk, deliberately left unchanged: raising it to ~1.27 m is the intended value but changes navigable width everywhere. |
